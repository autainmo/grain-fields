# 01 — Scene analysis

Analyze before naming a style. The goal is to discover which visual relationship can carry the reconstruction.

## Step 1 — Classify the source

Record:

- mode: photograph, illustration, graphic, collage, render, or mixed;
- content class: portrait, group, animal, action, architecture, interior, landscape, product, still life, sport, abstract, or mixed;
- aspect ratio and intended crop;
- existing text and whether it is factual, branded, decorative, or absent;
- current visual quality: clean, noisy, low contrast, compressed, underexposed, or already stylized.

Do not treat low technical quality as permission to invent detail.

## Step 2 — Find anchors

### Primary anchor

The element whose identity, action, or form carries the image.

It may be:

- one face or body;
- a group configuration;
- an animal or moving object;
- a product silhouette;
- a building, room, horizon, landform, or light event;
- a geometric or color relationship in an abstract work.

### Secondary anchors

Elements needed to understand context, scale, action, or direction.

### Identity-bearing details

List the smallest details whose loss would change recognition:

- eyes, mouth, hands, pose, species markers;
- logo, label, interface, product edges;
- architectural openings and perspective;
- ball, racket, wheel, tool, instrument, or contact point;
- horizon, reflection axis, shadow direction;
- exact text and numerical facts.

## Step 3 — Detect candidate relations

Evaluate these relation bases:

- semantic: subject-to-context meaning;
- geometry: silhouette, plane, axis, curve, enclosure;
- motion: trajectory, impact, wake, gesture, flow;
- light: highlights, shadows, glow, value planes;
- material: surface transitions, grain, transparency;
- depth: foreground-to-background scale and atmospheric loss;
- repetition: modular elements or natural rhythms;
- reflection: mirrored, refracted, or distorted continuity;
- perspective: vanishing direction and spatial convergence;
- contour: echoes and offsets around a form;
- negative space: empty area that isolates or points toward an anchor.

Choose the strongest one, or a primary plus one supporting relation.

## Step 4 — Define visual fields

A visual field is a coherent area governed by one relation and one mark behavior. Define one to four fields only when the image needs them. Do not split a continuous subject for quota compliance.

Each field needs:

- role: primary effect, support, transition, or quiet;
- relation basis;
- spatial extent;
- origin and direction;
- density and scale behavior;
- boundary logic;
- allowed overlap with protected content.

Fields may be nested, continuous, interrupted, radial, directional, planar, or depth-dependent.

## Step 5 — Score candidates

Score each candidate relation from 0 to 2:

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scene specificity | generic | partly specific | inseparable from this scene |
| Geometric support | contradicted | plausible | clearly visible |
| Continuity | fragmented | repairable | naturally continuous |
| Identity safety | high risk | manageable | reinforces identity |
| Thumbnail legibility | disappears | uncertain | immediately readable |
| Type-space compatibility | conflicts | workable | supports composition |

Prefer the highest total. A candidate scoring 0 on identity safety cannot be the primary field.

## Content-class guidance

| Source | Strong starting relations | Typical protected core | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portrait | contour, light planes, gaze direction, negative space | face, expression, hands, hairline | texture replacing facial structure |
| Group | repetition, spacing, shared gesture, depth | faces, body count, group topology | merging people into one texture mass |
| Animal or action | motion, wake, trajectory, contour echo | head, species markers, pose, contact point | effect detached from action |
| Architecture or interior | perspective, grid, plane, light, repetition | openings, load-bearing edges, vanishing logic | decorative mosaic flattening depth |
| Landscape | depth, horizon, weather flow, light, natural rhythm | horizon, major landform, landmark | no anchor and uniform texture |
| Product or still life | silhouette, material transition, reflection, shadow | geometry, logo, label, functional parts | product becomes an invented object |
| Sport | trajectory, impact, court or field geometry, repetition | athletes, equipment, ball, score | action center obscured |
| Abstract | rhythm, proportion, color adjacency, negative space | defining geometry and color logic | arbitrary effects with no source logic |

## Typography opportunity

Choose one:

- none;
- exact source text only;
- headline;
- caption;
- metadata.

Text must occupy real negative space and support hierarchy. Do not add a poster title merely because previous examples used one.

## Internal analysis output

Produce:

- source summary;
- primary and secondary anchors;
- protected identity details;
- candidate relations and scores;
- selected relation basis;
- one-to-four-field map;
- type-space decision;
- initial visual thesis;
- top three risks.

Do not expose this analysis unless the user asks for it.
